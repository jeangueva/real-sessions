# Lede

The sentence under a title: what the block is, in one paragraph. Larger than
the body's text and lighter than the title, so the eye reads the title, then
this, and then the block.

## Anatomy

1. **Paragraph** — a `p`, in the dimmer ink.
2. **Size** — 18px on a phone, 20px from `sm` up.
3. **Balance** — `text-pretty`, so the last line is never one word alone.

## When to use it

- The paragraph under a title, when the block needs a sentence to introduce
  it.
- The paragraph under the title of a `Widget`.

## When not to use it

- **The title of the block** → a `SectionTitle`: the lede is the sentence
  under it, never the title.
- **A hint under a form control** → a `Field`'s own hint, next to the
  control.
- **A message about what just happened** → a `Notice`: the lede introduces,
  the notice answers.

## Interaction

None. A paragraph is read, not reached.

## Writing

- One or two sentences, in the tú form: _Pago único, sin suscripción.
  Empieza gratis._
- What the reader gets, in the first sentence.
- 40 words on a phone, 60 on a desktop. A third sentence is a paragraph,
  not a lede.

## Accessibility

- The dimmer ink on the surface behind it: over 4.5:1 in both themes.
- It wraps and grows; it is never clamped, so the words under the title are
  all of them.

## Do and don't

- ✅ Say what the reader gets.
- ✅ Leave a question at the end only if the page answers it further down.
- ❌ Repeat the title in the lede.
- ❌ Use it for the copy inside a block: that copy belongs to the block.
